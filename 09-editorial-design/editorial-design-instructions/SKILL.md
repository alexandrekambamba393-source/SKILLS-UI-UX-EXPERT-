# Editorial Design: Design Instructions

## Core principle
Treat the page as a publication. Typography carries the voice, images carry the emotion, and pacing (the rhythm of text and visuals) guides the reader through the story. Reading comfort is the foundation.

## Typography
1. Choose a serif or a characterful sans for headlines and a highly legible text face for body copy. Pair them deliberately; do not use more than two families.
2. Headline scale is dramatic: display sizes at 48px to 96px on desktop, with tight leading (0.95 to 1.1).
3. Body text: 18px to 21px on desktop, line-height 1.6 to 1.75, 60 to 75 characters per line.
4. Use an optical hierarchy: kicker (small caps or uppercase label), headline, deck (standfirst), byline, body.
5. Drop caps and pull quotes add rhythm; use each at most once per article section.

## Layout and pacing
- Use a 12-column grid with a narrow text column (about 680px) centred or offset, and wider columns for images.
- Alternate text and visual blocks. Full-bleed images break long text and set the pace.
- Keep generous vertical spacing between sections (96px to 160px on desktop) to separate ideas.
- Captions sit close to their images and are visibly smaller and quieter than body text.

## Imagery
- Images are chosen for narrative, not decoration. Each one should advance the story.
- Use consistent crop ratios within a single article (for example 3:2 or 16:9) so the page feels edited.
- Provide meaningful alt text for informative images; mark purely decorative images as empty alt.

## Accessibility and reading
- Text contrast 4.5:1 minimum; large headlines at 3:1 minimum.
- Use real heading levels: one h1 per article, then h2 and h3 in order.
- Keep links distinct from body text using underline and a clear hover state.
- Support text resizing up to 200 percent without loss of content.

## Use and avoid
Use for news, magazines, blogs, long-form reports, brand stories and case studies. Avoid for transactional screens, dashboards or dense product interfaces.

## Anti-patterns
Full-width body text, more than two typefaces, decorative fonts for long paragraphs, images that repeat the text without adding meaning, and missing heading hierarchy.
