# Brutalism (Neo-Brutalism): Design Instructions

## Core principle
Show the structure. Borders, grids and type are exposed instead of hidden behind soft effects. The style is loud by intent, but every loud element must still communicate a clear hierarchy.

## Visual vocabulary
1. Borders: 2px to 4px solid black (or the ink colour) on every interactive and container element.
2. Shadows: hard offset shadows with zero blur (for example 4px 4px 0 #000). Never blurred shadows.
3. Radius: 0 or very small (at most 4px). Rounded corners contradict the style.
4. Palette: a limited set of saturated accents (two to four) on a neutral background, usually off-white or yellow. Use colour as a signal, not decoration.
5. Typography: a heavy grotesque or monospace family. Headlines at very large sizes (72px and up on desktop) with tight tracking. Body remains readable at 16px or more.
6. Layout: visible grids, hard dividers, asymmetry, and deliberate misalignment within a clear system.

## Interaction
- Hover: translate the element up and left to reduce its shadow, or invert colours. Make the shadow shrink, not disappear.
- Active: remove the shadow and translate the element into its offset, so the press feels mechanical.
- Focus: a thick outline (3px or more) in a contrasting accent.

## Readability and accessibility
- Body text contrast must reach 4.5:1. Saturated accents on saturated accents usually fail; place text on white or black.
- Do not use more than two accent colours in one view.
- Heavy type is for headings; long paragraphs should not be set in a heavy weight.
- Avoid relying on colour alone to distinguish states; pair with icons, labels or borders.

## Use and avoid
Use for portfolios, creative agencies, editorial sites, indie products and campaigns. Avoid for banking, healthcare, government or any task where calm and trust are the priority.

## Anti-patterns
Soft shadows or gradients inside a brutalist layout, rounded pills everywhere, more than two accent colours, tiny text in heavy weights, and decoration that adds no meaning.
